I have a folder called `graveyard/` with forty-something projects that never
shipped. I visit it more often than the repos with stars.

## What unfinished things taught me

- A storage engine I abandoned at 4k LOC taught me more about page layout than any book.
- A distributed scheduler that never scheduled anything taught me how leases fail.
- A chat bot that died in a week taught me to read an RFC before trusting a library.

The shipped projects taught me how to finish. The unshipped ones taught me how
to think — because they were never slowed down by users, compatibility, or the
fear of breaking something that someone depends on.

## The graveyard is a library

Unfinished code is not debt if you never paid interest on it. It is the closest
thing engineers have to a sketchbook: the place where you try the expensive idea
because nothing is at stake.

The only real rule is to write down, in the README, *why it stopped*. That note
is the difference between a lesson and a folder you never open again.
